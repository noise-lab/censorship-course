# AI, Copyright, and the Matcher

## Part 1 (in class, 20 minutes): Can the system tell?

Tuesday's question was how automated copyright matching works and what it can
and cannot see. Today the content is machine-made. In pairs, with a laptop, run
three quick experiments and write down what you find.

1. **The memorization test (the GEMA question).** The Munich court held that
   ChatGPT reproducing song lyrics verbatim was a copyright reproduction, and
   ordered OpenAI not to do it for GEMA-represented artists without a licence.
   Ask two different chatbots for the full lyrics of (a) a well-known
   English-language song and (b) a German song by Herbert Grönemeyer or Helene
   Fischer. Note what each one does: refuses, summarizes, quotes a line, or
   recites. Then ask for the first paragraph of a famous novel. Is the behavior
   a rule about lyrics, about copyright, or about a particular rightsholder?
2. **The provenance test.** Generate or find an AI-made image (any image model,
   or an image you suspect is synthetic) and check it at
   [Content Credentials Verify](https://verify.contentauthenticity.org/). Then try
   a photograph from a news site. Does either carry C2PA provenance? Now find a
   YouTube video that shows the "altered or synthetic content" label and one
   obviously AI-generated video that does not. Who decided the label: the
   uploader, the platform, or a detector?
3. **The reference test.** Content ID needs a reference recording to match
   against. Search YouTube or Spotify for a song you know was made with Suno or
   Udio (search "Suno" plus a genre). Is it labelled as AI? Is it monetized? If
   the model was trained on licensed catalogues (the Warner and Universal deals),
   who is the rightsholder of what it produces, and what would a Content ID
   reference even be?

**Report back**, one pair at a time: the one result that surprised you, and
whether it moves you toward "the matcher can handle AI content" or "the matcher
was built for a world that no longer exists".

## Part 2 (take-home, optional): what the platforms say they do

Read one platform's current policy on AI-generated content (YouTube's
[AI page](https://www.youtube.com/howyoutubeworks/ai/), Spotify's AI policy,
or Meta's AI labels) and write three sentences on what it detects, what it
asks the uploader to self-declare, and what happens when neither works.
