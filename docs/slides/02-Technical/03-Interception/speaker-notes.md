| 1 | **When the Wire Goes Dark** | • Recall Ch. 2 so far: DNS answers, TCP resets, the SNI. Each one reads something on the wire.<br>• Ask the class: once all of it is encrypted, where does the censor go? Collect guesses before showing the three bullets.<br>• Three places: the trust anchor (the device's root store), the endpoint (the app or phone), distribution (the app store).<br>• Your framing from earlier in the term: control moves from content to metadata to platforms. This section is the middle of that move. |
| 2 | **What Encryption Leaves Exposed** | • **Read first:** [RFC 9849, TLS Encrypted Client Hello (Mar 2026)](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9849.html).<br>• TLS 1.3, [RFC 8446](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc8446) (Aug 2018), encrypts the server certificate; SNI stays in the clear.<br>• DoH, [RFC 8484](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc8484) (Oct 2018).<br>• ECH needs its config from DNS ([RFC 9848](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9848/)), so a censor of encrypted DNS stops ECH too: China and Iran do this ([Niere et al., FOCI 2025](https://www.petsymposium.org/foci/2025/foci-2025-0016.pdf)); Russia's TSPU drops Cloudflare ECH outright (Nov 2024, throttling lecture).<br>• Land the gap: every row trusts the endpoints and the root store. |
| 3 | **How Your Browser Decides to Trust** | • Your DNSSEC demo: open the OS keychain, show the root list. "You have to start by trusting something."<br>• The four root programs: Mozilla, Apple, Google (Chrome), Microsoft.<br>• Any trusted root can sign for any name: one bad root breaks everyone.<br>• Adding a root is a supported feature: that is how enterprise inspection and antivirus work. It is also the state's way in. |
| 4 | **Interception Is Already Routine** | • **Read first:** [Durumeric et al., "The Security Impact of HTTPS Interception," NDSS 2017](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/ndss2017_04A-4_Durumeric_paper_0.pdf).<br>• ~8 billion handshakes. Intercepted: 4.0% Firefox update, 6.2% e-commerce, 10.9% U.S. Cloudflare; more than an order of magnitude above earlier estimates.<br>• Less secure after interception: 97% (Firefox), 32% (e-commerce), 54% (Cloudflare). 62% of middlebox connections less secure; 58% severely vulnerable.<br>• Method: proxy's TLS ClientHello doesn't match the browser named in the User-Agent.<br>• Ask: when a school or employer does this, is it censorship? |
| 5 | **Kazakhstan, 2019: A National Root** | • **Read first:** [Raman et al., "Investigating Large Scale HTTPS Interception in Kazakhstan," IMC 2020](https://censoredplanet.org/assets/Kazakhstan.pdf).<br>• Started July 17, 2019; root "Qaznet Trust Network," pushed by ISPs (SMS to subscribers) "for security."<br>• Legal basis dates to 2015 (operators required to pass encrypted traffic using a "security certificate").<br>• Triggered by SNI for 37 of the Alexa top 10,000: 20 Google, 7 Facebook-affiliated, plus Mail.Ru/VK. Paths through two sets of hops in AS9198 (Kazakhtelecom). 7–24% of 6,736 hosts affected.<br>• Intermittent for 21 days; off Aug 7, 2019, called a pilot, "to be used again when there is a threat."<br>• Browsers, Aug 21, 2019: [Mozilla](https://blog.mozilla.org/security/2019/08/21/protecting-our-users-in-kazakhstan/), [Google](https://security.googleblog.com/2019/08/protecting-chrome-users-in-kazakhstan.html), Apple (statement to [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/19/08/21/apple-google-mozilla-take-steps-to-block-kazakhstan-government-surveillance)).<br>• Measurement angle: done remotely, by setting the SNI and looking at the certificate returned. |
| 6 | **Kazakhstan, 2020: The Browser Veto** | • **Read first:** [Mozilla, "Mozilla and Others Block Kazakhstan Root" (Dec 18, 2020)](https://blog.mozilla.org/netpolicy/2020/12/18/kazakhstan-root-2020/).<br>• Dec 6, 2020: "Cyber Security Nur-Sultan 2020" exercise with the National Security Committee; new root (ISCA); Censored Planet saw interception active about one day.<br>• Mozilla, Google, Apple, Microsoft all blocked it.<br>• No later Kazakh attempt found in searches through Nov 2026; Mozilla's [support page](https://support.mozilla.org/en-US/kb/certificate-cannot-be-trusted-warning-kazakhstan) still describes the block.<br>• Why manual: [Chromium security FAQ](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/HEAD/docs/security/faq.md) — "Chrome does not perform pin validation when the certificate chain chains up to a private trust anchor." [Chrome's CT policy](https://googlechrome.github.io/CertificateTransparency/site_operators.html) exempts locally trusted and enterprise CAs.<br>• Ask: four companies decided a national policy would fail. Comfortable with that? |
| 7 | **Other States, Other Routes** | • **Read first:** [EFF on the Mauritius proposal (Apr 30, 2021)](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2021/04/proposed-new-internet-law-mauritius-raises-serious-human-rights-concerns); [BleepingComputer on Russia's CA (Mar 2022)](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/russia-creates-its-own-tls-certificate-authority-to-bypass-sanctions/).<br>• Mauritius: ICTA consultation Apr 14, 2021; proxy to "segregate" social media traffic, decrypt, re-encrypt, archive; comments due May 5, 2021. Mozilla and Google filed a joint objection ([Techdirt, May 19, 2021](https://www.techdirt.com/2021/05/19/mozilla-google-ask-mauritius-govt-to-abandon-plan-to-intercept-decrypt-all-social-media-traffic-originating-country/)). The [Internet Society](https://www.internetsociety.org/resources/internet-fragmentation/mauritius-ictas-threat-to-encryption/) says it was removed from consideration; no official withdrawal found.<br>• Russia: Mar 2022, Ministry of Digital Development's National Certification Authority; trusted by Yandex Browser (and Atom, discontinued 2024); not in Mozilla, Chrome, Apple, Microsoft stores.<br>• Vignette: Aug 3, 2026, VTB, Sber, Alfa-Bank, Rosselkhozbank, Promsvyazbank, Uralsib, Bank Saint Petersburg ([iXBT, Aug 4, 2026](https://www.ixbt.com/news/2026/08/04/chrome-i-safari-perestali-otkryvat-sajty-rossijskih-bankov--vsjo-delo-v-sertifikatah-bezopasnosti.html); [RIA, Aug 4, 2026](https://ria.ru/20260804/brauzery-2108832797.html)). iXBT names GlobalSign revoking certs for tens of thousands of Russian domains in June. Ministry told users on Aug 8 to install the state certs.<br>• Say: the stated reason is sanctions, not interception; the mechanism is identical. |
| 8 | **Catching a Rogue Certificate** | • **Read first:** [Mozilla, "Fraudulent \*.google.com Certificate" (Aug 29, 2011)](https://blog.mozilla.org/security/2011/08/29/fraudulent-google-com-certificate/); [Google, "Maintaining digital certificate security" (Mar 2015, updated Apr 1)](https://security.googleblog.com/2015/03/maintaining-digital-certificate-security.html).<br>• DigiNotar: fraudulent cert used against users in Iran, found Aug 2011 when an Iranian Chrome user posted the pinning error; browsers distrusted DigiNotar; bankrupt Sept 20, 2011.<br>• CNNIC: an intermediate issued to MCS Holdings (Egypt) was put in a MITM proxy; Google (Apr 1, 2015) and Mozilla (Apr 2) stopped trusting CNNIC roots.<br>• CT: [Chrome requires CT](https://github.com/GoogleChrome/CertificateTransparency/blob/main/README.md) for certificates issued after Apr 30, 2018 (enforced from Chrome 68, July 2018).<br>• Limit: all of this polices public CAs, not a root the user installs. |
| 9 | **When the App Is the Censor** | • **Read first:** [Citizen Lab, "Censored Contagion" (Mar 3, 2020)](https://citizenlab.ca/2020/03/censored-contagion-how-information-on-the-coronavirus-is-managed-on-chinese-social-media/) — the clearest statement of client-side vs. server-side.<br>• TOM-Skype: Villeneuve, ["Breaching Trust" (Oct 1, 2008)](https://citizenlab.ca/wp-content/uploads/2017/05/breachingtrust-1.pdf); keyword-matched chats uploaded to insecure servers; 166,766 messages archived.<br>• Knockel, Crandall, Saia, "Three Researchers, Five Conjectures," FOCI 2011: decrypted TOM-Skype keyword lists.<br>• [Every Rose Has Its Thorn (FOCI 2015, Aug 10)](https://citizenlab.ca/2015/08/every-rose-has-its-thorn/): YY, 9158, Sina Show, GuaGua; lists embedded in the client; surveillance capability on YY.<br>• YY added 45 coronavirus keywords Dec 31, 2019; WeChat: 516 COVID keyword combinations Jan 1–Feb 15, 2020.<br>• Land: invisible on the network, because the traffic is encrypted to the company. |
| 10 | **WeChat: One App, Two Systems** | • **Read first:** [Citizen Lab, "One App, Two Systems" (Nov 30, 2016)](https://citizenlab.ca/2016/11/wechat-china-censorship-one-app-two-systems/).<br>• Filtering only for accounts registered to mainland China numbers; persists after linking a foreign number.<br>• Server-side; keyword combinations; more in group chat; no notice to sender or recipient.<br>• Images: "(Can't) Picture This 2" (2019), hash matching and OCR. ["We Chat, They Watch" (May 2020)](https://citizenlab.ca/2020/05/we-chat-they-watch/): files between international accounts used to build the China filter.<br>• Scale: 1,439 million combined Weixin and WeChat MAU at June 30, 2026 ([Tencent Q2 2026 results](https://www.prnewswire.com/apac/news-releases/tencent-announces-2026-second-quarter-results-302849608.html)).<br>• Ask: if you message family in China, which system are you in? |
| 11 | **Super-Apps and Mini-Programs** | • **Read first:** [China Briefing on the MIIT app and mini-program filing rule](https://www.china-briefing.com/news/china-mini-programs-app-registration-end-march-2024/); [InterSecLab, "The Max Messenger" (Sept 24, 2026; v1.2 Oct 22)](https://interseclab.org/research/max/).<br>• MIIT notice 工信部信管〔2023〕105号, published Aug 4, 2023; covers apps and mini-programs (WeChat, Alipay); new ones from Sept 1, 2023, existing by Mar 31, 2024. Unfiled apps: no distribution or preinstall; removal from stores, blocking. Apps hosted outside mainland China cannot file.<br>• Possible link to Apple's Apr 2024 removals (next-but-one slide), per China tech experts quoted then.<br>• Max: preinstall mandate from Sept 1, 2025 ([Izvestia, Aug 2025](https://en.iz.ru/en/1932247/2025-08-06/max-messenger-will-be-mandatory-install-new-smartphones-starting-september-1)); RuStore preinstall extended to iOS the same date. InterSecLab: no E2E, server-set behavior per account, links flagged for inspection, stops working with an on-device VPN; no evidence found of use against specific people.<br>• Honest gap: no published measurement of censorship *inside* mini-programs was found. Prior work is privacy ([Citizen Lab 2023](https://citizenlab.ca/2023/06/privacy-in-the-wechat-ecosystem-explained/)) and security.<br>• Your take: the host is a second app store, and a state regulates one host instead of millions of developers. |
| 12 | **Filtering Beyond Chat** | • **Read first:** [Citizen Lab, "Missing Links" (Apr 26, 2023)](https://citizenlab.ca/2023/04/a-comparison-of-search-censorship-in-china/).<br>• Eight platforms: Baidu, Baidu Zhidao, Bilibili, Bing, Douyin, Jingdong, Sogou, Weibo. 60,000+ rules. Bing's political rules broader than Baidu's; for censored queries Bing shows only authorized (state) sources.<br>• [Engrave Danger (Aug 18, 2021)](https://citizenlab.ca/2021/08/engrave-danger-an-analysis-of-apple-engraving-censorship-across-six-regions/): 1,045 keywords in mainland China (458 political); 174 of those in Hong Kong, 29 in Taiwan.<br>• Xiaomi: Lithuania NCSC, reported Sept 21–22, 2021 (Mi 10T, file "MiAdBlacklistConfig," 449 Chinese terms, off for EU but remotely switchable). Xiaomi denied; [German BSI found no evidence](https://www.techradar.com/news/german-security-watchdog-finds-no-evidence-of-xiaomi-censorship). Present as contested.<br>• Discussion: is a foreign company filtering to stay in the market different? |
| 13 | **App Stores as a Lever** | • **Read first:** [GreatFire, "About those 674 apps" (Nov 2017)](https://en.greatfire.org/blog/2017/nov/about-those-674-apps-apple-censored-china); [The Record on Russia (Jul 2024)](https://therecord.media/apple-app-store-russia-vpns).<br>• China: removals around Jul 29, 2017 after rules requiring VPN authorization; Cruz–Leahy letter Oct 17, 2017; Apple's reply: 674 VPN apps removed in 2017. [AppleCensorship](https://applecensorship.com/news/unveiling-the-extent-of-vpn-app-removals-by-apple-from-the-russia-app-store-an-analysis-of-silent-removals-and-the-need-for-transparency): 98 VPN apps unavailable at Sept 16, 2024.<br>• [Meduza, Mar 28, 2026](https://meduza.io/news/2026/03/28/apple-udalila-iz-rossiyskogo-app-store-neskolko-vpn-klientov-i-prilozheniy-dlya-obhoda-blokirovok): Streisand, V2Box, v2RayTun, Razze, Happ removed; Article 15.1.<br>• Google contrast: [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/vpn/vpn-privacy-security/russia-demands-212-vpns-are-removed-from-the-play-store-but-google-is-resisting) reports 212 orders Mar–Apr 2025, few complied with.
