| 3 | **What Makes the Same Law Work Differently?** | • **Read first:** [HRW, "Russia: New Law Expands Government Control Online" (Oct 31, 2019)](https://www.hrw.org/news/2019/10/31/russia-new-law-expands-government-control-online), which judges the sovereign-Internet law "overly broad and vague" against the international-law standard of precision.<br>• Two columns = the frame for every country slide: vague offences, and no independent recourse.<br>• Callback to "Law Taxes Speech Without Banning It": vagueness makes the chill do the work.<br>• Say in your own words: is vagueness a bug these laws tolerate, or the feature they are written for? |
| 4 | **Law Built the Great Firewall** | • **Read first:** [1996 Interim Provisions, English translation (China Copyright and Media)](https://chinacopyrightandmedia.wordpress.com/1996/02/01/provisional-management-regulations-for-the-international-connection-of-computer-information-networks-of-the-peoples-republic-of-china/); [Cybersecurity Law, DigiChina translation](https://digichina.stanford.edu/work/translation-cybersecurity-law-of-the-peoples-republic-of-china-effective-june-1-2017/) (Arts. 50, 58).<br>• Art. 6 (State Council Decree 195, Feb 1, 1996): international traffic only through state-provided channels. Art. 14 is the penalty clause cited when people are fined for "climbing the wall"; enforcement has mostly hit VPN sellers, not users ([China Digital Times, Apr 2026](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/2026/04/translation-scaling-the-wall-youve-crossed-the-line/)).<br>• Jan 2017 MIIT notice (信管函〔2017〕32号): no cross-border channels, VPNs included, without approval; multinationals may lease lines from licensed carriers.<br>• Callback to the GFW slides (intro and protocols lectures): there we saw *how* it filters; here, *why it is legal*.<br>• Point: the gateway rule predates the firewall technology. |
| 5 | **The Cybersecurity Law Makes Every Operator a Censor** | • **Read first:** [DigiChina translation of the Cybersecurity Law](https://digichina.stanford.edu/work/translation-cybersecurity-law-of-the-peoples-republic-of-china-effective-june-1-2017/) (Arts. 12, 24, 37, 47); on the amendment, [Latham & Watkins summary](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/chinas-cybersecurity-law-amendments-increase-penalties-broaden-extraterritorial-enforcement).<br>• Adopted Nov 7, 2016; in force June 1, 2017. Amended Oct 28, 2025; in force Jan 1, 2026.<br>• Compare Art. 47 with the DSA: the EU platform acts on notice; here it must look and act on its own (liability plus a monitoring duty).<br>• Real-name: Freedom House says China has required it since at least 2012; Art. 24 made it statute.<br>• The 2026 amendment left operator duties unchanged and raised the stakes (fines, extraterritorial reach, Party-leadership article).<br>• Ask: if you ran a foreign platform, which article would you fear most? |
| 6 | **The Model Extends to AI and Identity** | • **Read first:** [Interim Measures for Generative AI Services, China Law Translate](https://www.chinalawtranslate.com/en/generative-ai-interim/) (Art. 4); [Library of Congress, "China: Centralized Internet ID System Officially Launched" (July 2025)](https://www.loc.gov/item/global-legal-monitor/2025-07-09/china-centralized-internet-id-system-officially-launched/).<br>• Gen-AI measures: issued July 10, 2023 by CAC and six agencies; in force Aug 15, 2023. Art. 4(1) binds providers *and* users.<br>• Cyberspace ID: measures issued May 2025; in force July 15, 2025; ID number + face scan; formally voluntary.<br>• Let students argue both framings: the state's (platforms collect less data) and critics' (one identity map, held centrally).<br>• Callback to the AI-as-information-arbiter material: here the "alignment target" is written in law. |
| 7 | **The "Sovereign Internet" Law** | • **Read first:** [HRW, Oct 31, 2019](https://www.hrw.org/news/2019/10/31/russia-new-law-expands-government-control-online); [HRW, "Disrupted, Throttled, and Blocked" (July 2025)](https://www.ecoi.net/en/file/local/2128121/russia0725+web.pdf).<br>• 90-FZ, dated May 1, 2019, in force Nov 1, 2019: ISPs must install state-supplied TSPU equipment; national DNS.<br>• Callbacks from the technical lectures: Twitter throttling (Mar 2021, the `t.co` over-match, Xue et al. IMC 2021); YouTube degraded from July 2024; ECH blocked Nov 2024.<br>• Point to make: China built the chokepoint in 1996; Russia retrofitted one onto a commercial, decentralized Internet by statute. |
| 8 | **Criminalizing Speakers and Readers** | • **Read first:** [Meduza, "Putin signs law penalizing online searches for content deemed 'extremist'" (July 31, 2025)](https://meduza.io/en/news/2025/07/31/putin-signs-law-penalizing-online-searches-for-content-deemed-extremist-by-russian-authorities).<br>• Mar 4, 2022, Art. 207.3: up to 3 years base; up to 10 aggravated; 10–15 with "grave consequences" ([TASS](https://tass.com/society/1417043)). Example: Ilya Yashin, 8.5 years (Dec 2022) for a video on Bucha.<br>• Foreign agents, 255-FZ (July 14, 2022; in force Dec 1, 2022): "under foreign influence" replaces the foreign-funding test ([HRW, Dec 1, 2022](https://www.hrw.org/news/2022/12/01/russia-new-restrictions-foreign-agents)).<br>• Sept 1, 2025: search fines 3,000–5,000 rubles; VPN-ad fines up to 500,000 rubles for companies. Minister Shadayev: prosecutors must prove the search was deliberate.<br>• Dec 2025 VPN-protocol blocking; 469 VPN services blocked by Feb 2026 ([HRW, Mar 12, 2026](https://www.hrw.org/news/2026/03/12/russia-digital-iron-curtain-falls-on-internet-freedom-protection-day)).<br>• Ask: who decides what is "knowingly false" about a war? |
| 9 | **Block the Foreign, Mandate the Domestic** | • **Read first:** [HRW, "Russia: Digital Iron Curtain Falls" (Mar 12, 2026)](https://www.hrw.org/news/2026/03/12/russia-digital-iron-curtain-falls-on-internet-freedom-protection-day); [Meduza, Telegram blocking reaches 95% (Apr 10, 2026)](https://meduza.io/en/news/2026/04/10/telegram-blocking-rate-in-russia-reaches-95).<br>• Max: national-messenger law June 2025; preinstall mandatory from Sept 1, 2025. Max reached 77.5M monthly users by Feb 2026 (HRW).<br>• Feb 10–12, 2026: YouTube, two WhatsApp domains, Facebook, Instagram, DW, RFE/RL removed from the national DNS. [Meduza (Feb 12)](https://meduza.io/en/brief/2026/02/12/this-was-russia-today) found the DNS removal leaky at first. CNN reported the Kremlin's confirmation of the WhatsApp block on Feb 12.<br>• FSB shutdown law: Feb 2026, no court order, grounds set by the president (HRW; Freedom House).<br>• Telegram: no formal ban; unusable without a VPN for most users since mid-March; a Levada poll found 49% still read Telegram channels.<br>• Ask: is a preinstall mandate a censorship law? |
| 10 | **Who Decides What Is Blocked** | • **Read first:** [Computer Crimes Law, UNODC English text](https://www.unodc.org/cld/uploads/res/document/computer-crimes-act_html/Computer_Crimes_Act.pdf) (Arts. 21–23); [CHRI, "Internet in Chains" (2014)](http://www.iranhumanrights.org/wp-content/uploads/Internet_report-En.pdf).<br>• The committee has 13 members under the Prosecutor General; the content list (9 categories, 78 items) is drawn from the CCL, the penal code, and the press law.<br>• Supreme Council of Cyberspace: created by the Supreme Leader in 2012; sets filtering policy and drives the NIN.<br>• Freedom House: shutdowns have no clear legal framework; decided by the Supreme National Security Council, carried out by the ICT ministry through ISPs.<br>• Say in your own words: for someone contesting a block, is "no law at all" worse or better than a vague law? |
| 11 | **From Protest Shutdowns to a Two-Tier Internet** | • **Read first:** [Freedom House, Iran: Freedom on the Net 2026](https://freedomhouse.org/country/iran/freedom-net/2026); [Article 19, *Tightening the Net 2020*](https://www.article19.org/ttn-iran-november-shutdown/) on the NIN; [OONI, Sept 2022](https://ooni.org/post/2022-iran-blocks-social-media-mahsa-amini-protests).<br>• Nov 2019: officials claimed no shutdown happened because NIN services stayed up; Article 19 counts it as one.<br>• Sept 21, 2022: WhatsApp and Instagram blocked (OONI); mobile curfews 4 pm–1 am (Article 19, 2023 review).<br>• Jan 8, 2026: Cloudflare saw traffic fall ~90% within 30 minutes ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/08/internet-collapses-in-iran-amid-protests-over-economic-crisis)).<br>• **Caution on dates:** Freedom House dates the ~88-day shutdown from late February; IranWire says March; Wikipedia treats Jan 8–May 26 as one blackout. Say "about 88 days, per Freedom House."<br>• "Internet Pro": ~5× price, consent to surveillance. Bridge to economic controls.<br>• Callback to "Not Every Shutdown Withdraws the Route" (June 2025). |
| 12 | **Copying the Template: Vietnam and Turkey** | • **Read first:** [VietnamNet, Decree 147 in force (Dec 25, 2024)](https://vietnamnet.vn/en/social-media-users-in-vn-must-verify-accounts-with-phone-numbers-starting-today-2356231.html); [HRW, "Turkey: Dangerous, Dystopian New Legal Amendments" (Oct 14, 2022)](https://www.hrw.org/news/2022/10/14/turkey-dangerous-dystopian-new-legal-amendments).<br>• Vietnam: issued Nov 9, 2024, replaces Decree 72; existing accounts had 90 days to verify; builds on the 2018 cybersecurity law.<br>• Turkey: Law 7253 (July 2020) added the local-representative rule (platforms with 1M+ daily users from Turkey); Law 7418 (Oct 2022) added Art. 217/A and extended throttling to takedown non-compliance.<br>• Article 19: major platforms set up local entities, so the 90% throttle was not imposed for that reason.<br>• Callback: Turkey's 2014 Twitter DNS block (the 8.8.8.8 graffiti).<br>• Ask: is a local-representative rule censorship? The DSA has one too. |
| 13 | **The Democratic Mirror: India** | • **Read first:** [Rödl & Partner, "Beyond the Draft: India's Amended IT Rules"](https://www.roedl.it/en-gb/it/insights/pages/tech-data-bites/2-26/beyond-draft-indias-amended-it-rules-synthetically-generated-information.aspx); Freedom House 2026 overview (row 1).<br>• G.S.R. 120(E), notified Feb 10, 2026, in force Feb 20, 2026: 3-hour action on government orders (was 36); labels for synthetic content.<br>• 65 shutdowns in 2025 is the Access Now figure from the intro lecture.<br>• Freedom House and Amnesty: the clock pushes platforms toward removal.<br>• Let the class argue before the comparison slide. |
| 14 | **Where Is the Line?** | • This slide is yours to argue; the cells are prompts, not answers.<br>• (1) If recourse is the line, is India's 3-hour rule closer to Vietnam than to the DSA?<br>• (2) Germany's NetzDG had a 24-hour clock (folded into the DSA, Feb 2024). Was it authoritarian? Why not?<br>• (3) Do these laws borrow democratic language on purpose? Russia cited fraud for the call blocks; China uses child-protection language against anonymity (Freedom House 2026).<br>• (4) What drives platform behaviour more: the law's text or the court behind it?<br>• Say where you come down. That sentence is the thesis of §4.1.1. |
